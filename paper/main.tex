\begin{abstract}
Urban air-quality forecasting is important for public-health warnings, environmental management, and city-scale decision support. Time-series foundation models have recently shown promising zero-shot forecasting capability, and TimeRAF demonstrates that retrieval augmentation can supplement a frozen forecasting backbone with useful historical knowledge. However, PM2.5 variation is strongly affected by local context such as meteorology, holidays, high-wind events, heavy rain, wildfire activity, and other environmental disturbances. This paper introduces Event-TimeRAF, an event-aware retrieval-augmented framework for Los Angeles County PM2.5 forecasting. Event-TimeRAF builds a source-preserving knowledge base from official air-quality, weather, calendar, and storm-event records; retrieves causally eligible historical windows; fuses retrieved evidence with tabular forecasters and a frozen Chronos-Bolt foundation model; and generates evidence-grounded explanations under an explicit drift audit. We evaluate the framework on EPA AirData PM2.5, NOAA Global Hourly weather, and hash-verified NOAA Storm Events records for 2019--2024. A lightweight drift-gated Event-TimeRAF variant obtains the strongest numerical result with MSE 26.111, MAE 3.116, RMSE 5.110, and $R^2$ 0.381. The strongest ungated supervised context baseline obtains MSE 26.185, while the full feature-level Event-TimeRAF XGBoost variant obtains MSE 26.712. The frozen Chronos-Bolt baseline obtains MSE 28.941, and retrieval-augmented Chronos reduces it to MSE 28.709. These results show that retrieval is beneficial in selected settings, especially for the frozen foundation-model fusion and drift-gated selection, but the MSE gain over the strongest context baseline is small and not statistically conclusive. Because NOAA Storm Events lacks machine-readable publication timestamps, event-aware results are reported as retrospective event-start sensitivity results rather than strict real-time event forecasts.
\end{abstract}
