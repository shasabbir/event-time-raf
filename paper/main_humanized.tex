\begin{abstract}
Pollutant persistence, meteorology, calendar effect, scarce environmental events and temporal distribution shift are all factors that need to be considered in short-horizon PM2.5 forecasting. When there is already a good local model (predictable part), it can be damaging to have a full analogue future retrieved by existing retrieval-augmented forecasters which can provide similar trajectories. In this study, TRACE-RAF, a Trust-gated Residual Analog Correction mechanism integrated in an auditable event-aware forecasting framework is proposed. A Source-Audited Context Builder brings alignment of EPA's official PM2.5 data with the NOAA GHCNh weather, calendar, and hash-verified NOAA Storm Events data. The Leakage-Safe Event-Context Retriever imposes a strict embargo on candidates, while TRACE-RAF retrieves the residual trajectories, instead of raw targets, that are out-of-fold. Each residual is scaled by an 18-variable reliability gate, which, based on retrieval quality, component disagreement, correction spread, event context, and drift evidence, increases or decreases the weight of the residual. Evaluation is done with a 168 hour lookback window, 24 hour horizon, 45,784 origins in the training set, 7,950 origins in the validation set, and a fixed final holdout of 2,460 origins in Los Angeles County between 2019 and 2025. TRACE-RAF obtains MSE 40.062, MAE 4.134, and RMSE 6.329. It is 4.63\% better than the XGBoost context model but only 0.07\% worse than the more powerful LightGBM context model, with MSE 39.144 for the Ridge regression (best overall). The TRACE-RAF decreases the MAE by 0.021 in comparison to the base ensemble, which is a nominal effect that is not significant after Holm correction. Event-conditioned and event-free TRACE-RAF variants are almost the same, while the event retrieval significantly worsens the Chronos-Bolt MAE after multiplicity adjustment. Results show selective residual correction and an auditable evaluation protocol are supported, but not uniform event or foundation-model gains. Even with the results being conditioned on events, Storm Events will continue to produce retrospective sensitivity analyses since it does not have machine-readable publication timings.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Because fine particulate matter (PM2.5) has respiratory and cardiovascular health impacts \cite{who2021airquality} it is a significant urban air-quality hazard. Good quality short-range PM2.5 forecasts can be used to aid public-health warnings, exposure minimisation, transport planning and environmental management. However, in Los Angeles County PM2.5 is not easily modeled based on pollutant history. Changes in concentration may occur due to wind speed, boundary-layer conditions, precipitation, seasonal variations, holidays, wildfire smoke and other unusual storm or high wind events. All these factors render the forecasting problem "temporal," and "contextual.

Pre-training TSFMs on vast time-series corpora and transferring the forecasting capability to new domains has recently been proposed as a way to enhance the generalization of forecasting. Examples are the TimesFM, Moirai, MOMENT, Chronos and Tiny Time Mixers \cite{das2024timesfm,woo2024moirai,goswami2024moment,ansari2024chronos,ekambaram2024ttm}. At the same time, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has demonstrated that non-parametric memory can be useful to foundation models for retrieving relevant external knowledge at inference time \cite{lewis2020rag,karpukhin2020dpr}. TimeRAF brings in these two concepts to zero-shot time-series forecasting by retrieval from a pre-selected curated knowledge base and insertion in a frozen TSFM via Channel Prompting \cite{zhang2025timeraf}.

One domain specific retrieval question, not completely solved by numerical time-series retrieval is air-quality forecasting. A historical PM2.5 window can be similar to the current window, but under different weather/effect conditions. On the other hand, a window that has moderate numerical similarity may provide more information if it has high wind or precipitation or holiday or stagnation or smoke context. This encourages a retrieval system that can incorporate source information records, weather, calendar data and the distribution-shift indicator as part of the evidence, not as the metadata.

This study proposes Residual Analog Correction for auditable event-aware PM2.5 forecasting mechanism under distribution shift (TRACE-RAF). The framework involves retrieval augmentation in an environmental forecasting scenario without being architecturally similar to that of TimeRAF's learned retriever and Channel Prompting mechanism. The four implemented modules are a Source-Audited Context Builder (SACB), a Leakage-Safe Event-Context Retriever (LSER), the TRACE residual-correction core and a Drift-Aware Forecast and Evidence Head (DFEH). They work together to answer four questions: how to put official records into retrieval-ready context; how to select candidates without "temporal leakage"; how analogue residuals can correct a good supervised forecast without being a substitute for it; how each forecast can retain evidence of retrieval, events, drift, and uncertainty.

These are the main contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Source-Audited Context Builder: a replicable procedure that is used to link EPA PM2.5, NOAA weather, calendar and hash-validated NOAA Storm Events records to maintain source identity, source coverage checks, and an explicit assumption of event-availability.
    \item Leakage-Safe Event-Context Retriever: a historical analogue mechanism integrating similarity in both PM2.5 shape and weather, calendar and event similarity after applying an embargo that every candidate target must end prior to the query lookback starting.
    \item An out-of-fold residual memory and validation-trained reliability gate to selectively apply analogue corrections to a convex LightGBM–XGBoost context ensemble: TRACE residual correction.
    \item Drift-Aware Forecast and Evidence Head: a layer of evaluation that provides direct, residual-corrected and frozen paths of Chronos-Bolt, transparent shift indicators, probabilistic diagnostics and machine-readable explanation evidence under one chronological protocol.
\end{itemize}

Evaluation is performed with a final chronological holdout and comprises linear, boosted tree, neural, retrieval-only, placebo-fusion and frozen-TSFM baselines. Paired inference is based on 168-hour blocks, 5,000 bootstrap resamples, HAC-corrected Diebold–Mariano tests and Holm adjustment over the declared comparison family. This design will ensure that negative evidence is as apparent as positive evidence and that event association is not inferred as causation.
\section{Literature Review and Related Work}

\subsection{Statistical, Machine-Learning, and Deep Forecasters}
There are various forecasting methods that vary in their trade of inductive bias, data required and computational effort. Autoregressive and/or seasonal models continue to be useful models as baselines since their failure modes are readily examined. This is extended to nonlinear lag, rolling, weather, and calendar interactions with the tree ensembles like XGBoost and LightGBM without the need for a sequence encoder \cite{chen2016xgboost,ke2017lightgbm}. RNNs with LSTM or GRU cells, however, are able to learn stateful temporal representations \cite{hochreiter1997lstm,cho2014gru}. However, systematic studies warn that recurrent networks are not straightforward alternatives to good statistical baselines, and must be well-preprocessed and evaluated \cite{hewamalage2021rnn}. DeepAR generalizes the recurrent model to also forecast a probability over related series \cite{salinas2020deepar}. Further large benchmark archives such as the Monash Forecasting Repository enable the reproducible comparison of forecasts in different classes of time-series domains \cite{godahewa2021monash}. Transformer families focus on dependencies of longer timeframes: Informer reduces the attention cost, Autoformer and FEDformer decompose the temporal variations, Crossformer and iTransformer model cross-variable structure, while TimesNet reorganizes temporal variation into two dimensional patterns \cite{zhou2021informer,wu2021autoformer,zhou2022fedformer,zhang2023crossformer,liu2024itransformer,wu2023timesnet}. The achievement of these gains does not necessarily mean that the level of architectural complexity needs to be increased. Simple decomposition or patch-based inductive biases are seen to be very competitive in DLinear and PatchTST \cite{zeng2023dlinear,nie2023patchtst} while a multiscale decomposition and mixing is used in TimeMixer \cite{wang2024timemixer}. Seasonal and boosted tree baselines are thus retained in the evaluation, not taking a deep backbone as the default winner. This option is in line with recent survey articles in the literature that state that model selection should be based on horizon and covariate structure, uncertainty requirements, and data size and scale, and not solely on architecture novelty \cite{lim2021survey,benidis2023deepforecasting}.

\subsection{Time-Series Foundation Models}
To avoid training a separate representation of each series, time-series foundation models (TSFMs) aim to transfer knowledge across datasets. Decoder-only forecasting design (TimesFM), universal forecasting Transformer (Moirai), multiple time-series forecasting tasks (MOMENT), value as token (Chronos), and small zero/few-shot transfer (Tiny Time Mixers) \cite{das2024timesfm,woo2024moirai,goswami2024moment,ansari2024chronos,ekambaram2024ttm}. Timer, Timer-XL, UniTime and Time-LLM investigate the concepts of generative pretraining, long-context scaling, cross-domain unification, or language-model reprogramming \cite{liu2024timer,liu2025timerxl,liu2024unitime,jin2024timellm}. Frozen pretrained models have also been tried as general computation engines in the time series analysis field \cite{zhou2023onefitsall}. All these studies inspire zero-shot evaluation, and provide evidence that transfer performance is related to the representation of the input and the shift of domains. Thus, in the present study, we compare the performance of frozen Chronos-Bolt to that of locally supervised models on a common set of forecast origins, instead of taking the superiority of the foundation models for granted.

\subsection{Retrieval-Augmented Forecasting}
In retrieval augmentation, the parametric knowledge is detached from an external memory. RAG and Dense Passage Retrieval are the early works that set the example of retrieve-then-condition in the field of natural-language processing \cite{lewis2020rag,karpukhin2020dpr}. More recently, the issue of retrieval has been explored in the context of diffusion forecasting and assisted generation workflows in time series \cite{liu2024ratd,ang2024tsgassist}. TimeRAF is the most relevant methodologically: it builds a time-series knowledge base, trains an MLP dual-encoder retriever, picks out the top-$k$ candidates and incorporates the embedding of candidates in a frozen TSFM via Channel Prompting \cite{zhang2025timeraf}. Its ablations split retrieval quality, integration, number of candidates, the composition of the knowledge base and backbone selection.

The current framework is also based on the principle of external memory, but it tackles another experimental question. It is based on transparent random, cosine and event-context similarity rather than a learned dual encoder. The main path of TRACE-RAF is to retrieve the historical model residuals and use a learnt trust gate; the other arm of TRACE-RAF, called Chronos-Bolt, is used for output space fusion but not for internal Channel Prompting. This therefore is not a copy of TimeRAF. An audited environmental adaptation to test the effectiveness of contextual analogues in providing additional information following a strong local forecast.

\subsection{Air-Quality Forecasting and Official Context}
The pollutants history with temperature, humidity, wind, precipitation and pressure are commonly used as variables to prepare PM2.5 forecasting. Recent reviews show that there has been a paradigm change from single-station recurrent models to attention-based, hybrid and spatiotemporal models \cite{zhou2024pm25review,zhang2023sparsepm25}. For instance, AirFormer performs spatiotemporal modeling in a deterministic way and uncertainty estimation in a stochastic manner at the nation level \cite{liang2023airformer}. Multi-station designs capture the spatial dependence, while the current study considers an aggregate at the county level, and the source of context evidence. EPA AirData provides PM2.5 observations that are regulated, NOAA GHCNh provides observations for hourly meteorology, and NOAA Storm Events provides structured hazard records \cite{epaairdata2026,noaaisd2026,noaastormevents2026}. Smoke evidence could be introduced in NOAA Hazard Mapping System products but they were not reported in the run \cite{noaahms2026}.

\subsection{Distribution Shift and Forecast Explanations}
Temporal nonstationarity can affect mean, variance and relationship between inputs and future values. Adaptive normalization methods react to this issue by calculating statistics on local temporal scales \cite{liu2023san}; TRACE-RAF makes use of interpretable shift indicators to audit regimes but without claiming to have removed shift. There are also variations in the scope of the explanation methods. SHAP can assign predictions to model inputs \cite{lundberg2017shap} and information-theoretic saliency can aid the counterfactual analysis of probabilistic forecasts \cite{raman2023forecast}. The narrower explanation layer implemented includes signed XGBoost effects, retrieved cases, event identifiers, drift components and an uncertainty proxy. The records provide information on traceability, and do not prove causes of the weather or events.
\section{Critical Gaps and Limitations of Previous Studies}

Four gaps in the event-sensitive urban air-quality forecasting are identified from previous studies. First, temporal forecasters and numerical retrieval systems do not always maintain the assumptions about provenance, coverage and availability that are made by local contextual records. A numerically similar PM2.5 window could be associated with a different weather or event, and the information associated with a retrospective event could be incorrect for forecast time. The SACB fills this void by bringing official records into the equation under explicit source, coverage and availability audits.

Second, if a candidate answer (either an input or a target) is present in the context of the query, historical retrieval may add "look-ahead bias. Just because it is similar, doesn't mean it can't be the same future in the retrieved evidence! The LSER tackles this deficit by imposing a strict temporal embargo and the training-history restriction, and then four separate terms of PM2.5 similarity, weather similarity, calendar similarity, and event similarity that can be audited independently.

Third, information provided by raw analogue futures can replicate or contradict that of a good supervised model. TRACE-RAF fills this void by saving only out-of-fold residual trajectories and training an origin level trust gate with an origin-level validation-only correction utility. Thus it does not pose the question of whether an analogue raw future should supplant the base forecast but asks whether an analogue explains the rest of the error.

Fourth, retrieval gains are reported without determining if they withstand distribution shift, simple forecast-averaging placebos, or can be attributed to stored evidence. To fill this void, the DFEH analyzes direct, residual-corrected, and frozen Chronos-Bolt paths on the same common origins, displays indicators of the shifts, and preserves evidence of the features, retrieval, events and uncertainties that are machine-readable. This component helps in comparison and auditability but not in providing causal explanation.

These maps are the boundaries of novelty of this study. TRACE-RAF does not include a learned dual-encoder retriever or internal Channel Prompting and the present experiment in a single county does not afford geographic generalization.

\section{Core Contributions}

The proposed framework offers four mechanisms which are in one-to-one relationship with the gaps above:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Source-Audited Context Builder (SACB): builds an aligned version of the context with 85 dimensions, based on official records of PM2.5, meteorological, calendar, and events, and preserving metadata of sources and availability.
    \item Leakage-Safe Event-Context Retriever (LSER): only allows retrieval of training-history analogues after enforcing a candidate-target embargo, and exposes the contribution of each similarity channel, and retrieved trajectory evidence.
    \item Trust-gated Residual Analog Correction (TRACE): builds an embargoed memory of forecast out-of-fold residuals, scales the retrieved corrections to the query scale and uses a reliability gate based on validation, instead of replacing all forecasts with residuals.
    \item Drift-Aware Forecast and Evidence Head (DFEH): combines all the above within one chronological evaluation contract.
\end{itemize}

Qualifications are made for the empirical contribution on purpose. TRACE-RAF improves on its context ensemble in nominal MAE inference and remains competitive with the best nonlinear baseline, but the family-wise Holm corrected component comparison revealed no verified all-origin effect for the event channel.

\section{Methodology}

TRACE-RAF comes as 4 inter-related modules. The Source-Audited Context Builder (SACB) takes heterogeneous official records, organizes them chronologically by the hour and preserves provenance and availability metadata. The Leakage-Safe Event-Context Retriever (LSER) builds up an embargoed historical memory and retrieves the analogues based on PM2.5, weather, calendar, and event similarity. The TRACE core transforms out-of-fold errors made by a supervised context ensemble into an analogue residual memory and establishes a notion of when to rely on that residual memory. The Drift-Aware Forecast and Evidence Head (DFEH) reveals the final forecast, the comparison paths, shift indicators and a machine-readable evidence record. No image operator, no internal TSFM Channel Prompting or Test Time Parameter Fitting (TTPF) is assumed for all the modules, which are based on one-dimensional time series or tabular features.

\subsection{Framework Overview and Forward Pass}

The entire dataflow is indicated in figure~\ref{fig:architecture}. The first phase is to load, audit and transform the EPA PM2.5 data and NOAA GHCNh, NOAA weather, calendar and NOAA Storm Events data into the SACB to form query contexts and input–target windows. The LSER takes the historical embargo first and then ranks those who meet eligibility requirements. Meanwhile, expanding window XGBoost and LightGBM models are built and strictly out-of-fold residuals are produced for the training memory. TRACE-RAF is able to retrieve and align those residuals, and estimate their reliability, while adding a gated correction to the context ensemble. The forecast, retrieval cases, event identifiers, drift evidence and uncertainty fields are recorded by the DFEH, while frozen Chronos-Bolt is an evaluated separately benchmarked path.

The entire mapping for every forecast origin $t$ is given by equation~\eqref{eq:event_timeraf_mapping}.
\begin{equation}
    \hat{Y}_{t+1:t+H} =
    F_{\theta,\psi}\bigl(\phi(X_t,W_t,C_t,E_t),G_t,D_t,\mathcal{R}\bigr),
    \label{eq:event_timeraf_mapping}
\end{equation}
Here, $\phi(\cdot)$ is the tabular feature construction, $G_t$ the retrieved historical evidence, $D_t$ the drift evidence and $\mathcal{R}$ the out-of-fold residual memory. The one methodological limitation is that all the candidate targets and all the residuals in $G_t$ and $\mathcal{R}$ terminate before the beginning of the lookback window for the query. The forward pass below is the expanded form of (5).

The implemented forward pass is extended to tensor notation in equation~\eqref{eq:forward_pass}, which is for a batch of $B$ origins.
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Q^B &= \operatorname{SACB}_{B}(X,W,C)\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times46},\\
Q &= [Q^B\,\|\,E]\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times85},\\
G^{r} &= \operatorname{LSER}(Q,\mathcal{R})
\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times8\times24},\\
(\hat{Y}^{L},\hat{Y}^{X}) &= \mathcal{H}(Q^B,C^{+})
\in(\mathbb{R}^{B\times24})^2,\\
\hat{Y}^{B} &= \eta\hat{Y}^{L}+(1-\eta)\hat{Y}^{X}
\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times24},\\
(\Delta,Z) &= \mathcal{A}(G^{r},\hat{Y}^{B},D)
\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times24}\!\times\!\mathbb{R}^{B\times18},\\
g &= \lambda\operatorname{clip}(h_{\psi}(Z),0,1)
\in[0,1]^{B\times1},\\
U &= \hat{Y}^{B}+g\odot\Delta\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times24},\\
(\hat{Y},\mathcal{O}) &= \operatorname{DFEH}(U,D,G^{r})
\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times24}\times\mathcal{E}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:forward_pass}
\end{equation}
Here, $\eta=0.6521$ is the out-of-fold-selected LightGBM weight, $\Delta$ is the aligned residual correction, $Z$ are 18 features that are target free and $\lambda=0.25$ is the validation-selected correction strength. $\mathcal{E}$ is the schema of evidence record $\mathcal{O}$. $\mathcal{H}$ and $\mathcal{A}$ are the paired context heads and residual-alignment stage. The 23 PM2.5 and 14 weather features and nine calendar features are in the $Q^B$ part, while the 39 event features are in the $Q$ part, and are not present in either base head. 9 known future-calendar terms are added to $Q^B$ for each horizon, which provides a total of 55 inputs to each base regressor. The feature-level M09 and frozen Chronos-Bolt arms remain a controlled comparison, and are not hidden within TRACE-RAF as per equation~\eqref{eq:forward_pass}.

\subsection{Source-Audited Context Builder}

The motivation behind the SACB is to maintain the data provenance and temporal availability before model fitting. As can be seen in Figure~\ref{fig:context_builder}, there are checks before the construction of the feature, which are specific to the source. EPA monitor records are consolidated into an hourly county median, NOAA weather data is synced from the weather surface station of choice, and event archives are hash checked to a manifest and calendar data is calculated from the local time stamp. The context is then windowed and broken down into chronological parts; no interpolation of target values.

\subsubsection{Dataset Construction}
Los Angeles County (LAC) hourly PM2.5 data is collected from the US EPA AirData/AQS parameter 88101 \cite{epaairdata2026}. If only one site meets the coverage criterion, then the data audit uses the same site for coverage and continuity. Since none of the monitors met this criteria the target is the hourly median of the monitors on the official Los Angeles County AQS network. Timestamps are converted to \texttt{America/Los\_Angeles}, daylight saving explicitly taken care of. Gaps in the input are only filled with observations from the past, in the case of a short gap no target is interpolated. The weather covariates are obtained from the closest appropriate NOAA Global Historical Climatology Network hourly (GHCNh) weather station \cite{noaaisd2026}. Calendar terms include cyclic hour, weekday, and month names, weekend, US Federal holidays, and CA holidays.

The concatenation of the feature groups at each origin is given by equation~\eqref{eq:context_vector}.
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
q^B_t&=[p_t\,\|\,w_t\,\|\,c_t]
\in\mathbb{R}^{23+14+9}=\mathbb{R}^{46},\\
q_t&=[p_t\,\|\,w_t\,\|\,c_t\,\|\,e_t]
\in\mathbb{R}^{23+14+9+39}=\mathbb{R}^{85}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:context_vector}
\end{equation}
where $p_t$, $w_t$, $c_t$ and $e_t$ are the PM2.5, weather, calendar and event feature vectors, respectively. Fixed lags, rolling moments and extrema, differences, current value and fill flag are included in the PM2.5 group. The weather group consists of 6 measured variables, a missing-flag for precipitation, flagged variables for the weather condition, a stagnation-flag and 24-hour rolling means of the weather variables. The event group includes one hour and active counts, 24/72/168-hour counts, category counts (72 hours) and event-burst ratio. Therefore, Equation~\eqref{eq:context_vector} sets both feature orderings; that of 46 dimensions (base context) for C01, M04, C05 and C06 and that of 85 dimensions (event-aware context) for LSER and event-conditioned controls.

Normalisation statistics are only fit on the training split. To prevent mixing of different scales affecting similarity, query and candidate windows are preprocessed in the same manner. The reported run contains 56,194 valid windows with $X\in\mathbb{R}^{56194\times168}$ and $Y\in\mathbb{R}^{56194\times24}$. Out of these, 45,784 are in the training set, 7,950 in the validation set and 2,460 are in the final test holdout set.

\subsection{Leakage-Safe Event-Context Retriever}

The LSER distinguishes between eligibility over time and relevance. The embargo is first displayed in figure~\ref{fig:retrieval_timeline}, a candidate is considered only if their complete target is before the start of query lookback. Then the list of eligible candidates is compared on four similarity channels which are separately stored. The futures that are selected correspond to the scale of the query, and are summarized for the forecasting head. This ordering avoids that a high similarity score is sufficient for this temporal constraint to be ignored.

\subsubsection{Time-Series Knowledge Base}
Historical windows of PM2.5 are available in the time-series knowledge base. All the information in each record includes a window identifier, start time, end time, input and future values, weather summary, calendar summary, normalized window vector, split, and normalization identifier. The Primary Knowledge Base is 24-hour origin stride and has 1,824 training windows. There are 6-hour and 1-hour strides in sensitivity arms, which give 7,608 and 45,784 candidates, respectively. The query-specific embargo in Equation~\eqref{eq:eligible_set} is not affected by candidate overlap, only the memory density.

Formally the knowledge base is defined as in equation~\eqref{eq:knowledge_base}.
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{K} = \{(u_i, X_i, Y_i, m_i, c_i, e_i)\}_{i=1}^{N},
    \label{eq:knowledge_base}
\end{equation}
where $u_i$ is a candidate origin, $X_i$ and $Y_i$ are candidate lookback and future windows, $m_i$ describes a weather summary, $c_i$ a calendar summary, and $e_i$ an event-context summary provided at $u_i$. The temporally eligible set of a query origin $t$ is defined in equation~\eqref{eq:eligible_set}.
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{A}_t =
    \{i: u_i + H < t-L+1,\; i \in \mathcal{T}_{train}\},
    \label{eq:eligible_set}
\end{equation}
To validate and test queries. The strict inequality in \eqref{eq:eligible_set} makes sure that the target period of a candidate ends before the query input period starts. Training-history condition \eqref{eq:eligible_set} is also taken into account for the final validation and the test retrieval. The stored candidate schema is defined in Equation~\eqref{eq:knowledge_base} and the admissible subset at query time is defined in Equation~\eqref{eq:eligible_set}.

\eqref{eq:retrieved_set} is the time-series retriever's result for query window $X_t$.
\begin{equation}
    R^{ts}_t = \{r^{ts}_{t,1}, r^{ts}_{t,2}, \ldots, r^{ts}_{t,k}\},
    \label{eq:retrieved_set}
\end{equation}
where $k$ is number of retrieved candidates). The default is $k=8$, with sensitivity analysis for $k\in\{1,4,8,16\}$. Equation~\eqref{eq:retrieved_set} is the returned records prior to summarizing future records.

\subsubsection{Event Knowledge Base}
Event knowledge base includes source-preserving information pertaining to Los Angeles County. The required structured source is NOAA Storm Events and the optional extension (not measured) is NOAA Hazard Mapping System fire and smoke records \cite{noaastormevents2026,noaahms2026}. Event identifier, start, end, information-availability assumption, location, category, source, source URL, title and summary are stored fields. Reported records include data for the following weather categories: wildfire, high wind, heavy rain, flood, and others; the data for the missing weather categories in the records are all zeros.

Model inputs include events counted every hour, events counted over a 24-, 72- and 168-hour window, events counted by category over a 72-hour window, events active and an event-burst ratio. The explanation evidence consists of the recent source identifiers. Target-horizon overlap is a post hoc subset label, and is never used as a model input. The publication time is not found in NOAA Storm Events and the implementation makes an assumption that the event start was available in a retrospective way. For strict operations experiments, records of real publication/issue time are required.

\subsubsection{Hybrid Ranking and Future Alignment}
The query window and candidate window of PM2.5 data are mean-centered and scaled by their standard deviations and normalized to a unit length. Weather and calendar groups are standardized and mapped to $[0,1]$ using the cosine similarity with candidate statistics. Explicit 0 vectors provided and event similarity clipped to 0-1. The hybrid retrieval score is represented by equation~\eqref{eq:hybrid_score}, which is a combination of time-series similarity, weather relevance, calendar relevance and event relevance.
\begin{equation}
    s(q, i) =
    \alpha s_{ts}(q,i) +
    \beta s_{w}(q,i) +
    \gamma s_{c}(q,i) +
    \delta s_{e}(q,i),
    \label{eq:hybrid_score}
\end{equation}
The time-series similarity ($s_{ts}$), the weather similarity ($s_w$), the calendar similarity ($s_c$) and the event relevance ($s_e$) are all included. The implemented weights are $\alpha=0.5$, $\beta=0.2$, $\gamma=0.1$, and $\delta=0.2$. To allow inspection of contribution from each relevance channel without a learned latent scoring function, Equation~\eqref{eq:hybrid_score} maintains the explicitness of each relevance channel.

The following equation~\eqref{eq:topk} is used to define a retrieved set.
\begin{equation}
    G_t = \operatorname{TopK}_{i \in \mathcal{A}_t}\; s(t,i).
    \label{eq:topk}
\end{equation}
$\mathcal{A}_t$ is applied to equation~\eqref{eq:topk} and then the equation is evaluated. How candidate futures are restored to the query scale is defined in equation~\eqref{eq:future_alignment}.
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{Y}_{t,i}=\mu_t+\sigma_t
\left(\frac{Y_i-\mu_i}{\max(\sigma_i,\epsilon)}\right),
\qquad i\in G_t,
\label{eq:future_alignment}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_t$, $\mu_i$ are the lookback statistics for the query and candidate respectively and $\sigma_t$, $\sigma_i$ are the respectively associated standard deviations. Retrieval-only forecasts consist of the averaged $\widetilde{Y}_{t,i}$ uniformly or by normalized scores that are all non-negative. The feature-level path preserves the mean trajectory, horizon-wise spread, mean and maximum time series similarity, and number of candidates, and is consequently a vector in $\mathbb{R}^{51}$, $\rho(G_t)\in\mathbb{R}^{51}$. The affine transformation that is expressed in Equation~\eqref{eq:future_alignment} will maintain the shape of the candidate trajectory while bringing the query back to its local scale.

\subsection{Trust-Gated Residual Analog Correction}

For TRACE-RAF, it's what you get back that changes. A raw candidate future is the future that is predictable based on the current context, plus the future that is not predictable based on the current context (the part the local model did not predict). Thus, if the future is added to a solid supervised forecast, the predictable structure can be double counted. TRACE-RAF, on the other hand, builds a memory of out-of-fold residual trajectories and generates trust coefficient of the retrieved correction at the origin level. This residual path from the two context models is reflected in the residual memory, trust gate, final forecast and evidence record as shown in figure~\ref{fig:explanation_flow}.

\subsubsection{Out-of-Fold Base Forecasts and Residual Memory}
The base forecast is a convex combination of the LightGBM and XGBoost context models as shown in equation~\eqref{eq:trace_base}.
\begin{equation}
\hat{Y}^{B}_t=\eta\hat{Y}^{L}_t+(1-\eta)\hat{Y}^{X}_t,
\qquad 0\leq\eta\leq1,
\label{eq:trace_base}
\end{equation}
where $\eta$ is only fitted from expanding window out of fold (OOF) training predictions. There are two OOF prediction blocks: $[0.50,0.75)$ and $[0.75,1.00)$ of the training sequence. Each of the fitting targets stops before the first of the prediction inputs for each block. The audit records 22,728 and 34,154 fit origins, 11,446 prediction origins per block, and a passed embargo, in both cases.

The stored residual for the out-of-fold candidate $i$ is given in equation~\eqref{eq:oof_residual}.
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_i=Y_i-\hat{Y}^{B,\mathrm{OOF}}_i\in\mathbb{R}^{H}.
\label{eq:oof_residual}
\end{equation}
The residuals are individually horizon-clipped at 0.01 and 0.99 quantiles of the training only data set. At 24-hour stride, 909 candidates are selected for validation while a total of 3,795 and 22,892 residual candidates are validated at 6-hour and 1-hour strides respectively. The last step is selected prior to test retrieval.

\subsubsection{Residual Retrieval and Scale Alignment}
LSER scores the candidates for the residual memory in the same way as is done in Equation~\eqref{eq:eligible_set}, based on the temporal eligibility. To aggregate the candidate residuals with the query scale, each $a_{t,i}$ is a non-negative retrieval weight, which is normalized, as shown in Equation~\eqref{eq:residual_alignment}.
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{t,h}=\sum_{i\in G_t}a_{t,i}
\frac{\sigma_t}{\max(\sigma_i,\epsilon)}\varepsilon_{i,h},
\qquad \sum_{i\in G_t}a_{t,i}=1.
\label{eq:residual_alignment}
\end{equation}
A spread vector is defined in a similar manner as the aligned residuals but is done on a horizon-wise basis. Unlike Equation~\eqref{eq:future_alignment}, Equation~\eqref{eq:residual_alignment} passes a history of the model error not a history of the target level.

\subsubsection{Reliability Gate}
An 18-dimensional target-free vector $z_t$ is given for each origin, summarizing the mean, standard deviation and maximum of the base-forecast, the correction, the correction-spread, the mean and maximum of the retrieval similarity, the number of selected and eligible candidates retrieved in the log space, the selected-event fraction, whether an event conditioning was used, the mean and maximum disagreement between LightGBM and XGBoost, and the continuous and binary drift indicators. The ideal scalar correction coefficient is defined in equation~\eqref{eq:oracle_gate} and is only used in the validation.
\begin{equation}
g_t^{*}=\operatorname{clip}\left(
\frac{\langle Y_t-\hat{Y}^{B}_t,\Delta_t\rangle}
{\|\Delta_t\|_2^2},0,1\right).
\label{eq:oracle_gate}
\end{equation}
This coefficient is learnt by a depth-2 histogram gradient-boosting regressor $h_{\psi}(z_t)$. The first 67\% of validation origins fit the provisional gate; the remaining 33\% select $\lambda\in\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1\}$. The chosen configuration: 24 hour stride in the residual memory and $\lambda=0.25$, the gate is then refitted, using the whole validation partition without test targets. The final forecast is given in equation~\eqref{eq:trace_forecast}.
\begin{equation}
\hat{Y}^{\mathrm{TRACE}}_t=\hat{Y}^{B}_t+
\lambda\operatorname{clip}(h_{\psi}(z_t),0,1)\Delta_t.
\label{eq:trace_forecast}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:oracle_gate} and \eqref{eq:trace_forecast} neatly de-couple the validation supervision from test time inference; it is required that $Y_t$ be present for training the gate, but it is not required to be present for all test reliability features.

\subsection{Drift-Aware Forecast and Evidence Head}

DFEH is the common layer for output and audit in both direct feature model and TRACE-RAF (validation only drift router) and the frozen Chronos-Bolt benchmark. The proposed path is a combination of the context ensemble and gated residual update as illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:explanation_flow}. A separate branch to maintain M09 feature level conditioning and Chronos-Bolt forecast fusion to test their effect without them getting mixed up with TRACE. The drift scores and flags stay as reliability input, subset definitions, evidence fields; the last TRACE gate is set prior to the test partition being opened.

\subsubsection{Feature-Level Forecasting and Drift Diagnostics}
The direct model input and model prediction for horizon $h$ is given by equation~\eqref{eq:direct_heads}.
\begin{equation}
u_{t,h}=[q_t\,\|\,\rho(G_t)\,\|\,d_t\,\|\,c_{t+h}],
\qquad \hat{y}^{X}_{t+h}=f_h(u_{t,h}),
\label{eq:direct_heads}
\end{equation}
For the M09 comparison model, $u_{t,h}\in\mathbb{R}^{153}$ for each $f_h$, which is an XGBoost regressor trained with a squared-error objective. This design allows for having a different supervised function for every forecast horizon and a common origin representation. The equation~\eqref{eq:direct_heads} is valid for the proved 153 characteristics: out of them 85 context and 53 retrieval, 6 drift and 9 future -calendar value. The drift score and binary flag are only used for the evidence record and regime analysis and are not adaptive forecaster selection in the experiment reported here.

\subsubsection{Frozen-TSFM Forecast Fusion}
The retrieval variant, called the Chronos-Bolt, fuses the forecast level instead of using internal Channel Prompting. Suppose that the frozen forecast Chronos $\hat{Y}^{C}_t$ and the historical retrieved forecast $\hat{Y}^{R}_t$ exist. The fused prediction is defined as in equation~\eqref{eq:chronos_fusion}.
\begin{equation}
    \hat{Y}^{CR}_t(\omega) =
    \omega \hat{Y}^{C}_t + (1-\omega)\hat{Y}^{R}_t,
    \label{eq:chronos_fusion}
\end{equation}
where $\omega \in \{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1.0\}$ is selected on the validation split. Validation selected $\omega=0.25$. This branch just does not change the embedding or weights for Chronos-Bolt as those changes are made after both of these forecasts are made. Thus, \eqref{eq:chronos_fusion} is not an internal TSFM prompting operation, it's an output space interpolation.

\subsubsection{Concept-Drift Evidence}
Implementations are not perfect concept drift identification, but distribution shift using transparent indicators. These indicators are: recent mean shift, recent variance shift, retrieval similarity drop, weather-pattern shift and event-burst ratio. The raw drift vector which is used by the code is defined by (21).
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
r_t=\bigg[&|\mu_{24,t}-\mu_{168,t}|,
\left|\log\frac{\sigma_{24,t}+\epsilon}{\sigma_{168,t}+\epsilon}\right|,
1-\operatorname{clip}(\bar{s}_{ts,t},0,1),\\
&\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\ell=1}^{4}
\left(\frac{w_{t,\ell}-\widetilde{w}^{tr}_{\ell}}
{s^{tr}_{w,\ell}}\right)^2},\ \max(b_{event,t},0)\bigg],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:drift_raw}
\end{equation}
The weather term is calculated using standardized temperature, relative air humidity, pressure and wind speed (standardized using training medians and standard deviations). The robust scaling of each component $r_{t,j}$ is carried out in equation~\eqref{eq:drift_scale} with the training median and median absolute deviation (MAD).
\begin{equation}
d_{t,j}=\frac{1}{6}\operatorname{clip}\left(
\left|\frac{r_{t,j}-\operatorname{med}_{tr}(r_j)}
{\max(1.4826\operatorname{MAD}_{tr}(r_j),\epsilon)}\right|,0,6\right).
\label{eq:drift_scale}
\end{equation}
The continuous score and binary flag is as defined in equation~\eqref{eq:drift_score}.
\begin{equation}
S_t=\frac{1}{5}\sum_{j=1}^{5}d_{t,j},\qquad
D_t=\mathbb{I}\!\left[S_t\geq
Q_{0.90}\!\left(\{S_v:v\in\mathcal{T}_{val}\}\right)\right].
\label{eq:drift_score}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:drift_raw} – \eqref{eq:drift_score} correspond to the detector implemented, with reference statistics set by data from the training set, the 0.90-quantile threshold determined by data from the validation set and the data from the test set transformed without refitting. The binary $D_t$ is kept for diagnostic regime analysis, and for explanation evidence.

\subsubsection{Evidence Record and Uncertainty Proxy}
The explanation module leverages signed local explanations from the 24 direct models, the weather flags, the top three retrieved windows, the recent event records, the calendar information, the forecast direction, and the drift evidence at the component level. It has its uncertainty proxy defined in equation~\eqref{eq:uncertainty_proxy}.
\begin{equation}
u_t^{\mathrm{proxy}}=
\sqrt{\bar{\sigma}_{R,t}^{2}+\bar{e}_{val}^{2}},
\label{eq:uncertainty_proxy}
\end{equation}
with the mean horizon-wise spread of the retrieved trajectories ($\bar{\sigma}_{R,t}$) and the validation residual RMSE ($\bar{e}_{val}$) respectively. The outcome is a diagnostic scale, a calibrated prediction interval is not produced. Each explanation is based on stored fields as opposed to an unrestricted language model and each event statement is still not causal. Only evidence-field definition of equation~\eqref{eq:uncertainty_proxy} is retained and not for the construction of the confidence interval.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics and Statistical Checks}
The main measurements are: MSE, MAE, RMSE, MAPE, sMAPE and $R^2$. MSE is kept as the main squared error metric and MAE and RMSE give more easily interpreted PM2.5 error scales. The following are the main error measures for $n$ observations $y_j$ and predictions $\hat{y}_j$.
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{MSE}&=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(y_j-\hat{y}_j)^2,\\
\operatorname{MAE}&=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|y_j-\hat{y}_j|,\\
\operatorname{RMSE}&=\sqrt{\operatorname{MSE}},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:error_metrics}
\end{equation}
The explained variance is summarized in equation~\eqref{eq:r2_metric}.
\begin{equation}
R^2=1-\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}(y_j-\hat{y}_j)^2}
{\sum_{j=1}^{n}(y_j-\bar{y})^2}.
\label{eq:r2_metric}
\end{equation}
Both equation~\eqref{eq:error_metrics} and \eqref{eq:r2_metric} are aggregated for all forecast origins and horizons to arrive at the overall result. The PM2.5 percentage errors (MAPE and sMAPE) are stored in the machine-readable output but are not used in the main ranking as percentage errors could be prone to instability at low PM2.5 values. Subset metrics are only displayed if there are 50 or more forecast origins. For the 72 hour event-context subset, 70 origins are included; for the drift subset, 407 origins are included, and for the 33 target-event and eight active-event origins, they are included in counts without being displayed in performance tables. Pairwise comparisons are based on 5,000 paired moving-block bootstrap resamples with 168-hour blocks, and Diebold–Mariano tests with 168 HAC lags. The $p$-values are stored both before and after Holm transformation. The thresholds for decisions based on the AQI are considered at 35.4 and 55.4 $\mu$g/m$^3$, while the thresholds for decisions based on the Chronos quantiles are considered by interval coverage, approximate quantile-grid CRPS, and calibration error.

\subsection{Explainability and Evidence Analysis}

In the context of DFEH, implement explainability as a source grounded evidence record not a post hoc free text interpretation. The run and window identifiers, origin time, retrieved historical windows, linked event records, leading feature effects, drift components, retrieval spread, validation residual RMSE, uncertainty proxy and drift flag, and the event-availability mode are stored in the pipeline for each of the 2,460 test origins. The resulting \texttt{explanations.parquet} file thus is in 1:1 relation with the forecasts evaluated. It's main local fields are typically current PM2.5 and recent rolling concentrations and analogue and event identifiers provide exposure of what evidence is available to the correction mechanism.

The information of the explanation layer is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:explainability_outputs}. Such a design is conducive to auditability: all explanatory statements are mapped to a model feature, a windowed retrieval time-series or an official event record, or one of the drift components. Does NOT prove causality: evidence of events is reported as contextual support and drift flag is reported as uncertainty warning. Faithfulness is assessed by varying complete sets of model features in the fitted M09 model. Table~\ref{tab:faithfulness} shows that PM2.5 history is by far the most influential and weather and calendar information are still influential. Event and retrieval permutations increase MSE while negative effects on MSE are found for the median-deletions. \section{Limitations}

First is the availability of events. NOAA Storm Events are official and are hash verified, and the public detail files do not give machine-readable publication times. For the SACB, the start-date of an event is thus a proxy for availability and event-conditioned results are retrospective sensitivity analyses and not operational forecasts.

The second restriction is the geographic validation. The experiment is for 1 county-level PM2.5 aggregate for 2019-2025. Based on the monitor-level analysis, the event-retrieval effects change sign between sites, although transfer to another county, climate and/or emissions regime cannot be determined.

The third restriction is on what is in the holdout. A total of 33 target-event origins and 37 exceedances of thresholds are detected, and no test origin exceeds 55.4 $\mu$g/m$^3$. The high error period in January 2025 is a period of validation and not external confirmations. Reporting of polluted smoke episodes or of operating alerts would thus be greater than the test support observed.

The fourth limitation is the construction of the targets. A County median makes it less likely that monitors are missing, but also less likely to show local peaks and is subject to the monitors who are active. The low coverage monitor arms detect some heterogeneity but are not dense enough to make inferences about events that comprise a subset of the measured events.

The fifth restriction is related to the empirical strength. The performance of TRACE-RAF is not superior to that of Ridge and both the residual-gate contrast and the event-channel contrast are not significant after Holm adjustment. Records of evidence are auditable, but the faithfulness perturbations of the event retrieval families are mixed and have no causal interpretation.

The sixth limitation is with regards to model integration. LSER is not a learned dual encoder, Chronos-Bolt is not an internal prompting backbone, and LSER is a transparent similarity retriever. The method should not be given as a copy of the TimeRAF Channel Prompting mechanism.

Lastly, hardware identifiers, peak memory and a git commit were not present within the run environment extract. Package versions, configuration hashes, archive hashes, and prediction-level verification provide better reproducibilities, and the execution time reported is not a hardware-normalized efficiency measure.

\section{Future Work}

Operational event studies should use archive that provides actual time of issue/publication instead of using event-start proxies and should include smoke-specific sources if the event transport of interest is for a wildfire. External validation to be carried out in a different US county with different meteorology and emissions and monitor density. Event effects then could be tested using spatial or site-aware forecasting. Future research could assess a learned residual retriever, richer embeddings of events, internally-conditioned TSFMs and calibrated exceedance probabilities. For each extension, a pre-registered external holdout must be retained and component-wise ablation should be carried out under the embargo of time.
\section{Conclusion}

In this study a method named as TRACE-RAF is presented for the auditable PM2.5 forecasting for a 24-hour period with introducing a trust gating approach for the residual analog error. SACB provides the harmonisation of the official PM2.5, GHCNh weather, calendar and Storm Events records, while LSER maintains a strict historical embargo; TRACE retrieves out-of-fold residual analogues and applies a learned reliability gate; and DFEH keeps prediction-level evidence and drift diagnostics. On the final 2025 holdout of 2,460 origins, TRACE-RAF obtains MSE 40.062, MAE 4.134, RMSE 6.329, and $R^2=0.444$. This boosts context XGBoost and is nominally better than the LightGBM comparator and Ridge is still best at MSE 39.144. None of the favorable TRACE comparisons pass Holm adjustment and the event-free TRACE ablation is virtually identical. So, the supported contribution is one that is reproducible, trust-gated, residual-retrieval and auditable evaluation, and does not require universal baseline superiority of retrieval with events and TSFM retrieval. Event issue times and geographic testing outside of the system, and smoke testing of events, will still be required prior to operational deployment.
