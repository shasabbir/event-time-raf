An Audited Evaluation of Event-Aware Retrieval for 24-hour PM2.5 Forecasting

ABSTRACT

While retrieval augmentation can provide a convenient method to provide forecasting models with historical analogues, the idea can be misleading when the memory is limited, when the comparison context is not very good, and when individual contextual channels are not tested independently. This study performs an audit of the Event-TimeRAF retrieval framework, which consists of a Source-Audited Context Builder, Leakage-Safe Event-Context Retriever and a Drift-Aware Forecast and Evidence Head. PM2.5 and NOAA Global Hourly weather data are aligned and calendar variables and hash-verified NOAA Storm Events data are aligned for Los Angeles County from 2019 to 2024. The lookback for evaluation is 168 hours, the horizon is 24 hours, the number of test origins is 7,199, the number of knowledge-base strides is four, the event-weight controls are used, and the retrieval placebos are applied, with overlap-aware paired inference. The weather-calendar XGBoost baseline model has the best performance as indicated by the MSE of 26.185, MAE of 3.125, RMSE of 5.117, and R² of 0.379. The full Event-TimeRAF model achieves the following MSE: 26.734, which is still not clear whether it differs from the baseline/context. Frozen Chronos-Bolt obtains MSE 28.941, retrieval fusion obtains 28.733, but a climatology fusion control obtains 27.450 and is much better than the retrieval fusion. What we see is that retrieval-only MSE is further enhanced with a denser six-hour memory (to 34.616), but that the feature model is still below the context baseline. An increase in the event weight results in 91.4% of the test top-k sets being changed, but also has a negative effect on retrieval error, suggesting that the tested event representation does not provide forecast value. Since Storm Events does not have publication time stamps, any findings are of retrospective sensitivity.

KEYWORDS

Forecasting, PM2.5, retrieval-augmented forecasting, time-series foundation models, event context, distribution-shift diagnostics, forecast traceability.

I. INTRODUCTION

Fine particulate matter (PM2.5) is one of the key urban air-quality risks as it is related to respiratory and cardiovascular health impacts [1]. Public-health alerts, exposure-reduction, transport planning and environmental operations can all benefit from accurate short-range PM2.5 forecasts. However, in Los Angeles County, PM2.5 dynamics are not as easily modeled using pollutant history. Concentrations may vary according to wind speeds, boundary-layer conditions, rain, seasonal activity, holidays, wildfire smoke, and unusual storms and/or high wind conditions. These factors render the problem of forecasting as being temporal and contextual.

Recent time-series foundation models (TSFMs) have been designed to enhance the generalization of forecasting, with the goal of pre-training on extensive time-series corpora and adapting the forecasting capability to other domains. Some examples of models are TimesFM, Moirai, the MOMENT, Chronos and Tiny Time Mixers [2]-[6]. Concurrently, retrieval-augmented generation (RA) has demonstrated how nonparametric memory can be leveraged to enable access to the relevant external knowledge at inference time for foundation models [7]-[8]. TimeRAF merges the two concepts for zero-shot time-series forecasting: retrieving sequences from a curated knowledge base that are relevant to the target sequence and passing them as input to a “frozen” TSFM using Channel Prompting [9].

The domain for air-quality forecasting introduces a retrieval problem which has not only a numerical time-series aspect but also a domain-specific aspect. A historical PM2.5 window is a window that is similar to the current window, but under other weather or event conditions. On the other hand, if the window is moderately similar in terms of numbers, but shares high wind, rain, holiday, smoke or stagnation context, then this window may provide more information. This encourages a retrieval system where source records, weather, calendar data, and distribution-shift indicators are all considered part of the forecasting evidence, and not just metadata unrelated to the forecast.

This work assesses Event-TimeRAF, a retrieval-augmented framework for PM2.5 forecasting under distribution shift, which is event-aware. The approach is an adaptation of the retrieval principle of TimeRAF in an official-source environment, and does not pretend to be reproducing the learned retriever or Channel Prompting of TimeRAF. The Event-TimeRAF consists of 3 named components: the Source-Audited Context Builder (SACB), Leakage-Safe Event-Context Retriever (LSER), and Drift-Aware Forecast and Evidence Head (DFEH). These components aim to answer three questions: how to map official environmental records to a retrieval-friendly context; how to choose historical candidates without temporal leakage; and when retrieval is beneficial in addition to a strong supervised weather-calendar baseline.

The contributions are the following:

- Source-Audited Context Builder: a reproducible mechanism that maintains EPA PM2.5 and NOAA weather (and calendar and hash-verified NOAA Storm Events) records while ensuring source identity, coverage checks, and the assumed event-availability.

With this in mind, the Leakage-Safe Event-Context Retriever, a historical analogue mechanism, is presented which enforces a query–candidate embargo, and is evaluated using memory density as well as neighbour count, event weight, and event-stratified controls.

Drift-Aware Forecast and Evidence Head: a controlled evaluation layer comparing direct XGBoost, retrieval fusion, non-retrieval fusion (placebos), while preserving the machine-readable shift and provenance evidence.

The assessment is made on a conservative basis. It only allows claims of a single manifest-backed run, presents 2,000-resample paired block intervals, presents overlap-aware Diebold–Mariano tests and does not presume that event association is the same as causation. The negative results differentiate a working retrieval pipe that can be audited from evidence of improved forecasting when retrieval occurs.

II. The existing literature and related works are reviewed.

A. Statistical, Machine-Learning and Deep Forecasters

Different forecasting methods trade off inductive bias, data requirements and computational requirements. The autoregressive and seasonal models continue to serve as informative baseline models, whose failure modes can be examined. Tree ensembles like XGBoost, LightGBM can be used to further expand this space to nonlinear lag, rolling, weather, and calendar interactions without a sequence encoder [10]-[11]. RNNs such as LSTMs and GRUs, however, learn a stateful temporal representation [12]-[13]. Large benchmark archives are also available, such as the Monash Forecasting Repository, which enables the possibility of being able to compare and reproduce results across different domains of time series [14]. For transformer families, they stress long-term dependencies, such as Informer that lowers attention expenses, Autoformer and FEDformer which decompose temporal variation, Crossformer and iTransformer which model the cross-variable structure, and TimesNet which reorganizes temporal variation into 2D patterns [15]-[20]. The benefits of these gains do not suggest that more complicated architecture is (always) required. Simple decomposition or patch-based inductive biases like DLinear and PatchTST can still be very competitive [21]-[22] while TimeMixer adopts explicit multiscale decomposition and mixing [23]. Event-TimeRAF is thus able to save seasonal and boosted tree baselines instead of assuming that a deep backbone is the default winner.

A) B. Time-Series Foundation Models

Time-series foundation models (TSFMs) aim to transfer knowledge between datasets instead of learning fresh representations for each time-series. TimesFM is a decoder-only forecasting design; Moirai is a forecasting Transformer that can be trained to forecast all tasks; MOMENT supports multiple time-series predictions; Chronos is a value-to-token forecasting model; and Tiny Time Mixers focuses on small zero and few-shot transfer [2]-[6]. Generative pretraining, long-context scaling, cross-domain unification or language-model reprogramming are the focus of Timer, Timer-XL, UniTime, and Time-LLM [24]–[27]. Frozen pretrained models have also been tested as generic time-series computation engines [28]. In total, these studies encourage zero-shot evaluation, while also demonstrating the need for input representation and domain shift on the transfer performance. The present study thus compares the forecast origins of frozen Chronos-Bolt to those of locally supervised models without making the assumption of superiority of foundation models.

C. Retrieval-Augmented Forecasting

Retrieval augmentation is the dissociation of the parametric knowledge from an external memory. The retrieve-then-condition paradigm was pioneered in NLP by RAG and Dense Passage Retrieval [7]-[8]. Diffusion forecasting and assisted generation workflow are used to study retrieval in time series. The most similar methodology is provided by TimeRAF [27] which builds a time-series knowledge base and trains an MLP dual-encoder retriever; then it selects the top-k candidates and introduces embeddings of the candidates into the frozen TSFM using Channel Prompting [9]. It ablations the retrieval quality, integration, number of candidates, knowledge-base composition, and backbone choice.

Event-TimeRAF is inspired by the external memory principle and answers to another experimental question. It is based on transparent random similarity, cosine similarity and event-context similarity instead of a learned dual encoder and feature- or forecast-level fusion instead of internal Channel Prompting. This difference does not allow the current implementation to be called a copy of TimeRAF, but rather is an audited environmental adaptation which will serve to test if contextual historical analogues enhance a local PM2.5 task.

Air-Quality Forecasting and Official Context.Air-Quality Forecasting and Official Context.

PM2.5 prediction is usually combined with the histories of pollutants, and with temperature, humidity, wind, precipitation and pressure. Recent reviews suggest a trend towards using attention-based, hybrid, and spatiotemporal models in place of single-station recurrent models [31]–[32]. For instance, AirFormer can isolate the deterministic spatiotemporal modelling from the stochastic uncertainty estimation at a national level [33]. These multi-station designs include a spatial dependence, while the present study deals with an aggregate at county level and with the origin of contextual evidence. EPA AirData provides regulated PM2.5 measurements, NOAA Integrated Surface Database provides hourly meteorological data, and NOAA Storm Events provides structured hazard data [34]-[36]. Smoke evidence could be added from NOAA Hazard Mapping System products, but were not included in the reported run [37].

E. Distribution Shift and Forecast Explanations

Means, variances and relationships between inputs and future values can change with temporal nonstationarity. Instead of inventing adaptive normalization methods, which estimate statistics at local temporal scales [38], Event-TimeRAF defines interpretable shift indicators to audit the regimes without claiming that shift has been eliminated. The range of explanation methods are also varied. SHAP assigns predictions to model inputs [39] and information-theoretic saliency helps with counterfactual inspection of probabilistic forecasts [40]. The explanation layer that was implemented is narrower, as it only contains signed XGBoost effects, retrieved cases, event identifiers, drift components and a diagnostic uncertainty scale. These records provide the basis for the traceability without providing any causal effects from events or weather.

III. There are some gaps in the literature and limitations of previous studies that must be taken into consideration.

There are three interrelated gaps in event sensitive urban air-quality forecasting that can be filled by the prior work. First, the assumption of local context records about provenance, coverage and availability as a whole may not be carried over to temporal forecasters and numerical retrieval systems. A numerically similar PM2.5 window may be brought about by a different weather or event regime and a retrospective event label may be misinterpreted as a piece of information at forecast time. The SACB fills this gap by putting the official data under explicit audits of source, coverage and availability.

Secondly, historical retrieval may suffer from look-ahead bias if there is an overlap of the query context with a candidate input or target. While it is true that the near-duplicate future cannot be duplicated into the evidence retrieved, similarity is not an issue. The LSER fills this void by means of a temporal embargo and a training-history restriction, which is followed by the separately auditable PM2.5, weather, calendar and event similarity terms.

Third, gains from retrieval are often not tested against a strong local context model which captures the useful signal, and whether they hold up under distribution shift, or an explanation can be traced to stored evidence. The DFEH tackles this shortcoming by comparing direct XGBoost and frozen Chronos-Bolt paths on typical origins, discovering shift indicators, and maintaining machine-readable feature, retrieval, event and uncertainty evidence. It is not an explanation of causation, but a component which enables comparisons and auditability.

These maps mark the boundaries of the new material in the study. Event-TimeRAF is not a learned dual-encoder retriever or equipped with an internal Channel Prompting, and the current experiment is limited to a single county, thus not directly demonstrating the ability to make geographic generalizations.

IV. CORE CONTRIBUTIONS

The proposed framework provides three mechanisms, which map 1:1 with the above gaps:

- Source-Audited Context Builder (SACB): creates an aligned 85-dimensional context with official PM2.5, meteorological, calendar and event records and metadata on the sources and availability.

- Leakage-Safe Event-Context Retriever (LSER): retrieve training-history analogues after enforcing a candidate-target embargo and expose the contribution of each similarity channel and the retrieved trajectory evidence.

Drift-Aware Forecast and Evidence Head (DFEH): This is one chronological evaluation contract for having direct horizon models, frozen-TSFM forecast fusion, drift-state analysis and traceable explanation records.

The empirical contribution is made deliberately tentative: while the weather-calendar context results in the most noticeable reduction in error, denser retrieval results in an improvement only in the retrieval-only path, and the event channel tested does not result in an improvement in forecast accuracy.

VI. METHODOLOGY

Event-TimeRAF consists of three main parts. The Source-Audited Context Builder (SACB) transforms heterogeneous official records into a chronological context of hours and adds provenance and availability metadata. The Leakage-Safe Event-Context Retriever (LSER) builds an embargoed historical memory, which it uses to retrieve analogues based on PM2.5, weather, calendar and event similarity. The Drift-Aware Forecast and Evidence Head (DFEH) reveals the feature-level, forecast-level prediction paths, indication of shifts and a machine-readable explanation record. These components are deterministic (or supervised) 1D time-series and tabular inputs, and not image operator, learned attention block or TimeRAF Channel Prompting.

A. Source-Context-Builder (Bachelorarbeit)

The SACB's driving force is to maintain data provenance and temporal availability prior to model fitting. Source-specific checks come before the construction of features as illustrated in Figure 2. Monitor data from EPA monitors are combined to a county hourly median, NOAA weather data is synced from the surface station selected, the event data archives are hash checked against a manifest, and the calendar data is calculated based on the local time stamp. The resulting context is then windowed, and chronologically broken, with no interpolation of the target values.

The event knowledge base comprises source-preserving information about events in Los Angeles County. D. Forecasting Task and Split

Forecasting task aims at forecast the PM2.5 for the next 1 to 24 hours with a lookback window of 168 hours. The split is chronological, the first 70% of windows for which we have a valid test set is used for training, the next 15% is used for validation and the last 15% is used for testing. The final test set has 7,199 origins of forecasts and 172,776 forecasted hourly target points. Test origins range from 7 February to 30 December 2024 UTC, while the test targets are up to 31 December 2024 UTC. Random splitting is not used.

D. Models

The models evaluated are the persistence, daily seasonal naive, weekly seasonal naive, hourly-by-month climatology, ridge regression, direct XGBoost models, random, cosine, calendar-only, and event-stratified retrieval models, the event-stratified retrieval models with and without drift features, the frozen Chronos-Bolt, and three variants of the Chronos fusion models that are optimized using validation. The fusion controls are used in place of retrieval, but they use climatology or persistence and maintain the weight-selection procedure. Ablation analysis is carried out with the help of an auxiliary random-retrieval XGBoost model (A01) and an event-free model (A00). A01 is a model that substitutes the cosine retrieval features with seeded random retrieval features, but keeps the context regressor. The A00 version doesn't include any event features at all, only a four-component drift diagnostic and event-free hybrid retrieval. The amazon/chronos-bolt-small checkpoint is used for the foundation-model evaluation. The assessed configurations are classified in table V in terms of their forecasting and retrieval functions.

VIII. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

A. Overall Comparison with Baselines: This section compares the overall score with the baseline score.

The results of the test set are shown in Table VIII. M04, which is based on PM2.5 history, weather and calendar features, is the best pre-specified supervised model with MSE 26.185, MAE 3.125, RMSE 5.117 and R² 0.379. The complete feature-level Event-TimeRAF model M09 achieves the following scores: MSE 26.734 and MAE 3.144. So, the full event, retrieval and drift feature set does not improve the all-origin error compared to the simpler context model.

A test of the frozen Chronos-Bolt was a success. A retrieval-fusion weight of 0.75 was chosen as the result of the validation. On the test set, M11 changes MSE from 28.941 to 28.733. The M11–M10 difference is -0.209 with a 95% block-bootstrap interval [−0.654, 0.216] and Holm-adjusted DM p = 1.000, so the change is unresolved. The fusion C10 with matched climatology results in MSE 27.450. M11 is worse than C10 by 1.283 MSE, with interval [0.694, 1.913] and adjusted p < 0.001. The persistence control is set to M10 by choosing the TSFM weight to be 1.0. This experiment thus does not identify a retrieval-specific advantage: the ordinary validation-tuned forecast combination does better.

C. Knowledge-Base Density & Event Sensitivity

Table XI demonstrates that the original sparse memory issue has implications only for retrieval. By decreasing the stride to six hours, the number of candidates becomes 5644 and the retrieval-only MSE becomes 34.616 from 191 and 39.037 respectively. One hour walk is not equal to six hours walk. Also, the separately trained full feature model gives the lowest observed MSE at six hours (26.412), but is still less than that of M04 (26.185). This means that dense retrieval enhances the historical analogue baseline, without changing the overall model ranking.

The event channel is active but is not helpful in the representation which is tested. At weight 0.2 it changes at least one of the top-8 candidates for 91.4% of test queries and it increases the event-candidate fraction for event-context queries from 32.9% to 95.7%. However, Table XII indicates that the all-origin and event-subset errors are greater the higher the event weight. There is also a drop in event-stratified retrieval as compared with the event-free ranking. The conclusion supported by this study is not that there is no forecast signal in environmental events, but rather that this count-based event representation and ranking rule is ineffective.

Fall F. Evidence and Traceability Analysis

In the DFEH, traceability is done as a source-embedded evidence record, instead of a post-hoc, free-text interpretation. The run identifier, window identifier, origin timestamp, retrieved historical evidence identifiers, event evidence identifiers, leading feature effects, drift components, retrieval spread, validation residual RMSE, diagnostic uncertainty scale, drift score, drift flag and event-availability mode are stored for each forecast origin evaluated. The evaluation resulted in 7,199 explanation records in explanations.parquet. A representative record shows the highest local contribution fields that are the current value of PM2.5 and recent rolling PM2.5 mean, associates the prediction to the retrieved 3 historical windows, associates the most recent source-recorded storm events (if they are present), and distribution shift when the drift detector is active.

An explanation layer is summarized in Table XIV. This design allows for auditability as every explanatory statement is linked to either a model feature or to a retrieved time-series window, official event record or drift component. It is not necessarily causal – evidence of event is reported as contextual support and the drift flag is an uncertainty warning.

The nominal interval of the Chronos-Bolt is 80% and it has 78.2% test target coverage with a mean width of 9.890 μg/m³. This interval result is not the same as the diagnostic_uncertainty_scale in the evidence record that is a combination of retrieval spread and validation residual RMSE and is not a calibrated interval.

The predominant interpretation is that there are strong conventional context features for this Los Angeles County task. Retrieval-only baseline is enhanced by dense historical memory, retrieval and event and drift features do not outperform M04. But the small m11 change vs frozen chronos is yet to be resolved and the climatology fusion control is much better than m11. Event-TimeRAF is thus evidence of a protocol for auditable evaluation and a scoped negative result, but not evidence for superiority of retrieval or of events.

Finally, all event-aware interpretations are to be read with the caveat that they are available. NOAA Storm Events records are officially and hash-verified, but the publication time is not provided in machine-readable format. For the experiment, the availability time is the event start time, therefore the results related to events are retrospective sensitivity results. A true study of the operation would involve real issue or publication time event or smoke sources, e.g. an audited NOAA HMS cache or alert product archive.

IX. LIMITATIONS

The first restriction is that of event availability. NOAA Storm Events records are official and hash verified; DOES NOT include machine-readable publication times in the public detail files. Thus, the results, conditioned on an event starting, are retrospective sensitivity analyses performed by the SACB and are not operational forecasts.

The second limitation is the geographic validation. The experiment spans an aggregate of PM2.5 for one county over the time period of 2019 to 2024. It does not claim transfer to another county, monitoring topology, climate or emissions regime, and thus is not a full-fledged generalization according to the external-validation criterion for a mature generalization claim.

The third constraint is related to the integration of models. The LSER is based on transparent similarity functions and the DFEH is a combination of Chronos-Bolt and retrieval only at forecast level. So, Event-TimeRAF does not duplicate TimeRAF's trained dual encoder or built-in Channel Prompting.

The fourth limitation is with respect to the reporting of computations. Runtime could not be interpreted as a hardware-normalized efficiency result as the run manifest was missing hardware model-identifiers and peak memory.

The fifth constraint is the aggregation of targets. The hourly county median is a combination of a varying group of monitors and is less affected by local extremes. This enhances the series continuity, but may distract from the association of events and highlight the inability to make conclusions regarding individual neighborhoods or monitoring sites.

X. FUTURE WORK

Operational event studies should use archives that actually provide the real time of the issue or publication, and should consider types of sources which are closer to PM2.5, such as smoke observations, in place of event-start proxies. Second US county with different meteorology and emissions should be done with the same protocol. Future research could test targets at the site level, a learned retriever for event-context, more expressive embeddings of events, calibrated probabilistic uncertainty, and conditioning in the TSFM. All source audit, temporal embargo and component-wise controls as present here should be maintained in each extension.

XI. CONCLUSION

This study audited event-aware retrieval for 24 hours forecasting in the case of PM2.5 in Los Angeles County. The pipeline synchronizes the official environmental data log, implements a temporal embargo based on queries and candidates, checks the memory density and sensitivity of the event-channel, and keeps an environmental record traceable. M04 is the strongest evaluated model, with MSE 26.185, MAE 3.125, RMSE 5.117, and R² 0.379; full M09 obtains MSE 26.734. M11's frozen Chronos-Bolt MSE drops to 28.733 (from 28.941), but the paired interval crosses zero, climatology fusion is at 27.450 and is much better than M11. Dense retrieval provides a better retrieval-only path than the tested event channel does. The contribution is therefore a replicable evaluation and a limited negative finding: Historical retrieval can be done under a verified query–candidate temporal embargo and audited extensively without necessarily providing forecast value in addition to robust local context and straightforward forecast combination.
