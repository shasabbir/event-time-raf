\section{Conclusion}

This paper presented Event-TimeRAF, an event-aware retrieval-augmented framework
for explainable Los Angeles County PM2.5 forecasting under distribution shift.
The implemented pipeline combines official EPA PM2.5 observations, NOAA weather,
hash-verified NOAA Storm Events records, leakage-safe time-series retrieval,
direct XGBoost forecasters, drift indicators, evidence-based explanations, and a
frozen Chronos-Bolt publication gate. In the final 2019--2024 Kaggle
experiment, the strongest all-origin model is the XGBoost weather-calendar
context baseline with MSE 26.185, while the full Event-TimeRAF XGBoost variant
obtains MSE 26.712. Retrieval improves the standalone cosine baseline over
random retrieval and gives a small favorable MSE change when fused with frozen
Chronos-Bolt, but the full feature-level design does not outperform the strongest
supervised context baseline. The study therefore positions Event-TimeRAF as an
auditable event-aware retrieval framework with mixed empirical effects, and it
identifies stricter event availability, external validation, and deeper
foundation-model integration as the main directions for the next version.
